The Freight Office builds trade runs from live prices: pick the cargo, the hull and the range — get the run. Nothing is planned until you submit the form.

1. "Starting system" — left blank, your last journal position is used.
2. "Cargo (t)" — what you can actually load. Typing in the box suggests hull capacities (Cobra Mk III 44 through Type-9 700 and Type-10 720).
3. "Go to load" (how far to fly for the pickup: 20–200 ly) and "Carry up to" (how far to haul it: 20–500 ly).
4. "Credits" — your budget; blank means no limit. "Commodity" — name one, or leave "best available".
5. "Rank by" — the three profit rankings: "Best trip" (total profit, the default), "Best per tonne", "Best per hour". Every card shows all three figures; this only chooses which leads the list.
6. "Prices seen" — default "Within a week". "Large pad only" and "Include fleet carriers" as needed. Press "Plan the run".

Each route card shows "Load at" and "Sell at" (system, distance, arrival distance, price), "Carrying" with why the tonnage is capped — "your hold", "supply at the pickup", "demand at the sale" or "your credits" — "Per tonne" with the up-front outlay, and "Per hour" with the estimated run time.

The time model is stated above the results: "Run times assume a ~22 ly laden jump, about 8 minutes per stop, and add supercruise time from each station's arrival distance when we hold it." The supercruise term grows with distance — roughly 4 extra minutes at 5,000 Ls, up to around 80 for a Hutton-length leg. Fuel stops are not modelled.

Two honest edges. If either leg's price is over 30 days old, the card says so: "check before you commit to the trip." And carriers, when included, appear as ordinary legs — the route cards do not badge them — so remember a carrier's prices are set by hand and it can be somewhere else tomorrow; that is why they are excluded until you tick the box. A banner also warns when the live price feed itself has stalled.
