---
title: How Data Bounty points are scored
surface: both
route: /bounties
---
A bounty's points are the days its station has gone unobserved — staleness-weighted by construction.

The base score:

- A station with market data: one point per day dark, capped at 1,825 days (five years).
- A station the squadron holds no market for at all: 1,000 points flat, listed as "never seen". Total darkness outranks most staleness.

Jackpots double it. A station qualifies as a jackpot when both are true: it sits in squadron space (within 200 ly of an active colonisation project), and it is either never seen or more than a year dark. Jackpot rows wear the "Jackpot ×2" chip and pay double — so a never-seen squadron-space station pays 2,000 points, and the theoretical maximum, five years dark in squadron space, pays 3,650.

The galaxy tail has no jackpots: its rows pay base points only, however dark. The tail exists for completeness — the stalest data anywhere — while the doubled money stays in the space the squadron actually flies.

The points shown on the board are the points paid: the board computes them when it rebuilds (every half hour), and the claim banks whatever the row showed. Points feed the Data Runners leaderboard and its season standings, and claims also count toward the achievement badges — "First Light" for your first claim, "Jackpot Hunter" for ten jackpot claims, "Cartographer" for claiming a never-seen station, and "Data Runner Champion" for topping a season.
