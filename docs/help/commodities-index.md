"Commodities" lists every commodity the squadron holds a price for — what everything is worth, right now, across the bubble. Prices are reported by commanders flying the game and by the galaxy-wide feed, and the page works signed out too.

The columns: "Commodity", "Category", "Avg buy", "Avg sell", "Margin" (average sell minus average buy, shown only when both sides are known), "Best buy" (the single cheapest market anywhere — the number to beat), "24h move" (movement in the average sell price over the last day), "Supply" and "Demand". The index figures update hourly, and fleet carriers are kept out of the averages entirely.

Two more columns — "Near buy" and "Near sell" — appear when the page knows where you are. "Near" means within 50 ly, and your position comes from one of two places:

1. A system you type into "Measure from system…" and confirm with "Measure". A typed system always wins, so you can plan tomorrow's run from your sofa, and the choice rides in the URL.
2. Otherwise, your ship's last known position from your own journal — where you last jumped or docked, via the companion app. The banner says which and how old: "Near-you prices measure from {system} ({station}) — where your ship last was, {age}." If that reading is a day old or more, it suggests naming your system instead.

With no position at all — signed out and nothing typed — the near columns simply do not render, and the table is galaxy-wide.

Search matches the commodity name; the category select filters; and "Sort by" offers "Best margin" (the default), "Highest sell price", "Highest buy price", "Cheapest to buy", "Biggest movers" (a 12% crash matters exactly as much as a 12% spike), the two near sorts when you have a position, "Most supply", "Most demand" and "Name". Rows link to the commodity's detail page.

The companion app carries a reduced version of the same table that re-reads itself every minute.
