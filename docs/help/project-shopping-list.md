A project's "Where to buy" tab prices out finishing the build: for each commodity still needed, where to buy it and what it will cost.

Reading the bars first: each commodity's bar has three segments — "delivered" (cyan), "aboard attached carriers" (yellow), and "still to source" (the empty track). The same legend applies on the "Needs" tab.

The table's columns are "Commodity", "To buy", "Cheapest at", "Price", "In stock" and "Cost". "To buy" is what remains after subtracting the attached carriers' effective holds — the sub-line "{n} t aboard" says how much was subtracted, and a fully covered line's station cell reads "already aboard the attached carriers — nothing to buy". When any carrier cover exists, a banner states it plainly and points you at the "Carriers" tab to declare or correct a hold.

Steering the suggestions:

1. "Buying from" — name a system; until you do, it defaults to the build's own system.
2. "Prefer" — "Local first" (the default), "Cheapest anywhere", or "Closest anywhere".
3. "Within" — 50, 100, 200 or 500 ly.
4. "Large pad only" — tick it if your hauler needs one. Press "Update".

"Local first" ranks by distance band, then by whether the price was seen within the last 90 days, then price. A price nobody has confirmed in 90 days sorts behind a believable one however cheap it looks — old colony-market prices have been measured to be wildly wrong. Every price carries its age ("seen today", "seen 3d ago"…); over 30 days is flagged. Fleet carriers are never suggested as places to buy — one that jumps away before you arrive is the worst possible entry.

A commodity nobody in range sells reads "nobody in range sells this", with the nearest known seller anywhere as a second line. The footer totals it: "Finishing this costs about {n} cr in cargo to buy", with honest caveats when lines are unsourced or fully covered.
