Two boards list what the squadron flies.

"Squadron builds" ("SQUADRON BUILDS — What people here are flying, and how they fitted it") lists every build a member has shared — squadron-visibility and public both — newest first. It is for members: "Open one to see the whole loadout and what it does — and to take it into the outfitter and change it for yourself."

"Public builds" ("PUBLIC BUILDS — Ships the squadron has published for anyone to use") lists only the publicly published ones. No account needed — this is the squadron's shop window, open to the web.

Each card shows the build's name, its ship and role — "Mining", "Combat", "Exploration", "Trading" or "Multipurpose", worked out from what is fitted rather than declared — four figures ("Jump", "Cargo", "Shields", "DPS"), and who shared it, when. The whole card opens the build.

A build's page is a read-only readout: the headline figures, then the full loadout by section — "Bulkheads", "Core internals", "Hardpoints", "Utility mounts", "Optional internals" — with empty slots omitted. The figures are the ones recorded when it was shared; the price is current. The link "Open the outfitter with a {Ship}" starts you on the same hull to build your own version — it opens the hull fresh rather than copying the loadout across.

The companion app carries both boards with the same cards, refreshing every minute. Its build reader goes one step further than the website's: it recomputes every figure and the price from the current module catalogue — the same arithmetic the outfitter runs — and says so, which is why its readout can differ from the snapshot on the card.

If nobody has shared anything yet, the squadron board says so plainly: "Nobody has shared a build yet. Fit one in the outfitter and choose who may see it — yours would be the first."
