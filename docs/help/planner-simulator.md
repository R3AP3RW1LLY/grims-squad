A plan's "Build order" tab walks the plan step by step and checks it against the game's rules.

Construction points come in two currencies, T2 and T3. Tier 1 builds earn T2 points; tier 2 builds spend T2 and earn T3; tier 3 builds spend T3. Each row shows its cost, what it earns, its tonnage with a running total, and the plan's T2/T3 balance at that step. The first site is the primary port — the game charges no construction points for it. Starports get dearer as a system fills: after the second non-primary starport, each further one pays a surcharge (a tier 3 port's cost climbs 6, 12, 18; tier 2 climbs 3, 5, 7), marked with an asterisk on the row.

When something cannot work, the banner "This plan cannot be built in this order" appears and each problem is spelled out: a step short of points ("Build something that earns tier {n} points before it."), a build whose prerequisite is not earlier in the order, or a slot with nothing chosen yet.

"What dropping this plan into the system does" sums seven figures across every step: "Population", "Max population", "Security", "Technology", "Wealth", "Standard of living", "Development". These are gathered by players, not published by Frontier, and are the least confirmed numbers on the page — a large starport really does cost the system security; that is the game, not a mistake.

Each starport and outpost also gets a predicted economy, worked out from its body (type, rings, terraformability, volcanism), from fixed-economy builds, and from what else stands on the same body feeding it — with the leading economy shown and the full audit in the hover. From that economy mix the planner predicts the station's market: "sells" and "buys" rows, major lines bold, minor lines trimmed on small pads and surface sites. Installations feed the port on their body and have no market of their own. The page states its blind spots — ring reserves, biological and geological signals, tidal lock — and that this is a model of unpublished rules, not a reading of a live market.
