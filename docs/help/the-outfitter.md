title: Outfitting a ship in the Outfitter
surface: both

The Outfitter (Shipyard → "Outfitter") fits any hull the game has with every module that fits it, and shows what the result actually does. Mass, jump range and the power budget update as you change things — nothing is submitted and nothing is saved until you want it to be. It works without an account; saving needs one.

1. "Choose a hull": search by name ("Ship"), filter by pad — "Any pad", "Small", "Medium", "Large". Prices shown are the hull alone, before anything is fitted.
2. Pick a "Starting point": "Factory stock" (the loadout the ship is sold with), "Combat", "Mining", "Explorer" or "Trader" — each fits the whole ship from the game's own module list. The screen opens on "Trader", fully outfitted; the moment you edit a slot, the chip reads "Edited".
3. Work the slots: "Bulkheads", then "Core internals", "Hardpoints", "Utility mounts" and "Optional internals", each section totalling its own mass, power and cost. Slot sizes sit beside each dropdown — a module larger than its slot is not offered, and a smaller one usually saves weight. "— empty —" clears a slot; an empty slot costs nothing and weighs nothing, which is often the right answer on a long-range build. The "i" button on any row shows the module's figures.

The sticky summary bar keeps the score: total cost (hull and fitted separately), then "Speed", "Boost", "Jump", "Power" (drawn against generated), "Shields", "Armour", "DPS", "Laden jump", "Cargo", "Fuel" and the weights. Draw more power than the plant makes and it says so: "This draws more power than the plant makes. Fit a larger plant, or power something down."

The Outfitter models the game's stock modules: engineering, power priorities and module switching are not part of the fit.

The companion app carries the same editor with the same presets and engine; its two tabs keep their state, so flipping to "AI assisted" mid-edit does not discard a half-fitted ship.
