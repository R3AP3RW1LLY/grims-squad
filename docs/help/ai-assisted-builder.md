The Shipyard's second tab, "AI assisted build", fits a ship to a job: tell it what the ship is for, then either name a hull or give it a budget. The fitting engine does the rest from the game's own data — so what it names is a ship you can walk into a shipyard and buy.

1. "What is the ship for?" — pick "Mining", "Combat", "Exploration" or "Trading". This decides what gets fitted and what gets sacrificed for it.
2. "Do you know which ship?" — two cards: "I have a ship in mind" (pick the hull, and it is fitted for the job as well as it can be fitted, without a spending limit) or "Tell me what to buy" (a budget search over every hull in the game).
3. On the budget path, "How much can you spend?" covers hull and modules together — "10 million", "50 million", "150 million", "400 million", "1 billion", or an exact figure. Nothing over it will be suggested, because a recommendation you cannot afford is worse than none. Press "Build it".

The result names the ship, its full cost against your budget, the figures ("Jump", "Laden jump", "Cargo", "Mass", "Shields", "Armour", "DPS", "Power"), an explanation of why that hull, and any compromises the budget forced. "Open in the builder" takes it onward — on the website that opens the hull in the outfitter; in the companion app it hands the entire computed loadout into the editor. "Start again" resets.

Every module comes from the game's own data and every figure is computed from what is fitted — nothing is guessed at. No language model is involved, so this keeps working even when the squadron's AI backend is down, and it is open to anyone who can use the Shipyard, signed in or not.

You can also just ask: GMSD AI answers "what should I fly for mining with 50m" with the same engine's result.
