"Planning" lays out a whole system before anybody flies anywhere: name a system and its bodies are drawn from what commanders have scanned, put builds on them, order them, and see what the whole thing costs.

To start a plan:

1. In "Start a plan", fill "System" and "Call it", and pick "Whose" — "Mine" or "The squadron's". Starting a plan takes the same rank as posting a project, and planning on the squadron's behalf is officers' work.
2. Press "Start planning". The bodies come from what commanders have scanned and shared; a system nobody has surveyed yet still gets a plan — it simply has nothing to draw until somebody scans it.

Your plans are private to you; squadron plans are everybody's. The page lists both under "Squadron plans" and "Your plans", each row showing its site count and total tonnage.

A plan has two tabs. "The system" is the body tree: each body's type, gravity, temperature and distance, and its build slots. Slots are two numbers per body — "orb" and "surf" — typed in by members who read them off the game, never predicted; any member can record them, and the row credits who did. Once a cap is recorded, the headings count against it ("orbital · 2 of 4") and the picker stops offering full slots.

"+ add a build" opens the catalogue picker, filterable by tier and pad, each option reading "{build} · T{tier} · {pad} pad · {tonnes} t". Sites are reordered with the ↑ and ↓ buttons; the first site in the order is the primary port — a fact about being first, not a checkbox — and the game charges no construction points for it.

Plans are versioned: if somebody else saves while you are editing, your save is refused with both revision numbers and you reload rather than silently overwriting them.

The "Build order" tab is the simulator — points, problems and system effects — covered in its own article.
