---
title: What it means when your post is held
surface: website
route: /forum
---
Every new thread, reply and edit is screened before it goes on the board. Almost everything passes straight through and publishes instantly. When the screener objects — or cannot be reached at all — the post is held instead of published, and the same thing happens in both cases.

What you see when held:

1. Your post does not appear in the thread.
2. The bell rings with "Your post is waiting for review": "It is not on the board yet. A reviewer will look at it and publish it, and you will hear the outcome here."

A reviewer — an officer with review permission — reads held posts oldest-first, with the standing instruction that the screener is often wrong and a false alarm costs a member's goodwill. They make one of two calls:

- Published: you are told "Your post is now on the board" — "A reviewer published your post in "{thread}"." The announcements the hold suppressed then fire normally: a held opening post announces its thread to the squadron on release, and a held reply notifies the thread's followers.
- Refused: you are told "Your post was not published" — "A reviewer looked at your post in "{thread}" and it will stay off the board. Speak to an officer if you would like to understand the decision."

Each decision also teaches the screener: a release tells it it was wrong, a refusal that it was right.

Two related states you may meet as a reader: locked threads are read-only for everybody, moderators included, with a banner pointing you at the help board; and a member under a posting restriction sees the reason, how long it lasts, and how to appeal when they try to post.

You can delete your own posts; deletion is soft, so a moderator can restore one removed in error.
